# Assessing a layout

Use these checks with any form, including forms not listed in the catalogue. They concern the arrangement of supplied content; they do not ask you to rewrite it or model relationships within it.

## Understand the supplied emphasis

Read enough of the supplied material to avoid misleading emphasis. Identify what it says, which audience or purpose the brief names, and which context, unit, source, or qualification must remain attached for the message to stay accurate. Use only priorities supported by the brief or material. If the intended priority is unclear and would change the layout, ask. If it would not change the layout, retain the supplied treatment or use equal emphasis.

For example, a supplied figure of **8%**, qualified by “among surveyed respondents,” should retain that qualification beside the figure. Giving 8% a dominant field may make it look more general or certain than the supplied evidence supports.

If two supplied claims have equal priority, enlarging one suggests a ranking that the source does not give. Keep them balanced, or ask which one should lead if the brief requires a clear primary message.

## Check the arrangement

- Does size, placement, grouping, or reading order express only the priority supported by the brief?
- Are captions, labels, units, sources, and qualifications readable and clearly associated with the content they explain?
- Are meaningful scales, grouping, and labels preserved? Does the arrangement work at its intended presentation size and phone size when relevant?
- Does the rendered output show clipping or overflow, or text that has become too small to read?
- Does content remain readable without JavaScript and in print where supported? Does the renderer’s keyboard navigation work?
- Would a change resolve a consequential ambiguity, or does the current layout already work?

Keep the current arrangement when it preserves the supplied meaning and remains readable. If space is insufficient, state the constraint for the author. Do not shorten prose, remove facts, invent content, or redesign a supplied item to force it into the layout. Record the sizes and modes reviewed, and note any verification gaps.
