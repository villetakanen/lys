# Kata

Kata is a kit of forms and practices for people and agents. Use this entry point
to navigate the shared material and apply it to the intended communication.

Read the [shared practices](references/practices.md), then the relevant form's
arrangements, examples, alternatives and limits:

| Intended relationship | Form |
| --- | --- |
| One subject should hold attention | [One focus](references/composition.md#one-focus) |
| Subjects deserve comparable consideration | [Equal peers](references/composition.md#equal-peers) |
| A supplied priority leads its context | [A lead with support](references/composition.md#a-lead-with-support) |

Choose for what the item should communicate and how it should feel. Adapt the
form to the actual material; consider a meaningful alternative. A form can also
explain why an existing arrangement should be retained. Combine forms where
useful. If none fits, explain the gap and use the shared practices without
forcing a named form.

Preserve supplied wording, quantities, uncertainty, associations and slide order.
Keep authored charts, tables and diagrams intact. Ask when missing intent or
context materially changes meaning; resolve routine placement decisions yourself.
The shared practices cover captions, labels, qualifications, headings and footers.

When implementing, read the [rendering integration](references/integration.md)
and the chosen renderer's authoring guidance. Deliver the finished artifact after
inspection. Explain consequential form choices and trade-offs briefly outside
audience content and speaker notes; extra variants are useful when requested or
when they help a decision. Report the viewing sizes and modes actually checked.
For a review request, apply the same forms and practices to the supplied work.
